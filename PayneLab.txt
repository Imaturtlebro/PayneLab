You’ve essentially jury-rigged a prototype for a **Max Payne-style active ragdoll stunt simulator** inside Bonelab using slow-motion and partial physics overrides. The reason it feels so satisfying—even with the jank—is that VR gunplay usually locks you into rigid, upright capsule colliders. The core issue you hit with the "floppy legs" boils down to how standard VR ragdolls work. When you toggle a ragdoll state, the game usually turns off or heavily slacks the joint motors, leaving your lower body as a limp chain of rigidbodies being dragged around by your hips. To make this feel intentional and heroic rather than like a wet noodle, you need **active ragdolls** paired with **procedural pose targeting**.

Here is a breakdown of how a dedicated hybrid mod could solve that physics puzzle and turn your experiment into a tight, responsive movement system:

**1. Active Ragdolls & Dynamic Motor Stiffness**
Instead of turning joint torque to zero or minimum, an active ragdoll applies **procedural spring torque (PD control)** to joint motors. During normal standing, motor stiffness is high to keep you upright. When initiating a slide, dive, or roll, you don't drop stiffness to zero; you dynamically lower it and feed the joints **target rotations**. This gives the legs enough internal structure to hold a shape (like a tuck or a leg extend) while still reacting dynamically if you hit an obstacle.

**2. Procedural Pose Targets for Movement States**
Rather than relying on pre-baked animations, you can define procedural pose states based on your velocity vector and surface normal:

* **The Slide:** When crouched at high forward velocity, the hips lower, the lead leg extends forward, and the trailing leg bends inward. Joints maintain medium stiffness to act as shock absorbers against the floor.
* **The Stunt Roll:** When triggering a dive while moving, the torso rotates around the center of mass, and the hip/knee joints automatically tuck toward the chest to reduce rotational inertia before dampening upon floor impact.
* **The Prone/Diving Peek:** The legs extend backward with low lateral stiffness, letting them trail behind your torso like a tactical slide into prone rather than collapsing into a pile.

**3. Center-of-Mass Impulses & Trajectory Control**
Right now, you're manipulating movement via hand/thumbstick inputs attempting to fight ragdoll gravity. A proper mod would apply a calculated physical impulse directly to the **Center of Mass (CoM)** on activation—launching the rigidbodies on an actual ballistic path—while locking or smoothing the VR camera height relative to the torso so you don't instantly get hit with motion sickness.

**4. Context-Aware Ground Collision & Dampening**
To stop legs from spazzing out on uneven geometry, the mod could raycast down to detect floor angles. When contact is made during a dive or slide, the lower-limb spring dampers temporarily absorb the impact force, then re-engage motor torque so you can seamlessly transition back into an upright stance or a stable seated fire position.

Hooking into Bonelab's Marrow engine via a custom C# MelonLoader assembly to dynamically modify joint spring parameters and pose targets would give you complete control over that transition from player-controlled IK to active ragdoll physics.

Swapping Ultimate Ragdoll to Left Stick (LS) and Flip Mod to Right Stick (RS) fixes the core control conflict immediately. LS handles overall spatial locomotion and ground movement, while RS acts as the dedicated stunt/rotation axis.

Making **ARM + Proc LEG** the default state changes everything. Instead of toggling between "100% stiff player avatar" and "100% limp ragdoll," the system becomes a dynamic continuum.

Here is how all these pieces fit together into a clean, state-driven physics framework:

---

### 1. Control Scheme & Mode Hierarchy

* **Default State: `ARM + Proc LEG**`
* **Upper Body:** Arms retain player IK / controller tracking with light joint dampening so you can still aim, shoot, and grab surfaces.
* **Lower Body:** Legs run active procedural IK target poses with variable PD motor torque (not limp weight, not rigid animation).


* **LS (Left Stick) — Ground & Spatial Steering:**
* **Standing/Air:** Drives directional center-of-mass momentum and sliding trajectories.
* **Prone/Downed:** Pushing LS left or right applies rotational torque along the spine axis to initiate ground barrel rolls.


* **RS (Right Stick) — Aerial Stunt Torque:**
* **In Air:** Pitch (LS forward/back) and Roll (LS left/right) trigger stunt flips around the torso’s center of mass.
* **Redundant Flip Mod Removed:** RS directly feeds target angular velocity to the pelvis rigidbody during airtime.



---

### 2. The Upright Stabilizer (Subtle Recovery Torque)

To stop the player from snapping upright instantly like an action figure, the stabilizer uses a **torque-capped PD spring**:

$$\tau = k_p \cdot \theta - k_d \cdot \omega$$

* **Soft Angular Spring ($k_p$):** Calculates the angle error $\theta$ between the spine's up-vector and `Vector3.up`. It applies a gentle rotational force to pull the chest upward.
* **Torque Ceiling:** Capping the maximum allowable force means high momentum (a hard hit, a fast flip, or a high-speed fall) easily overpowers the stabilizer.
* **Damping ($k_d$):** High rotational dampening prevents back-and-forth wobble, so when the body rights itself, it glides back into position smoothly instead of bouncing.

---

### 3. Procedural Leg Pose State Machine

Instead of static IK targets, the legs evaluate three vectors every tick: **Velocity**, **Torso Pitch/Roll**, and **Raycast Ground Proximity**.

| State | Trigger Conditions | Target Pose Configuration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Inverted / Air** | Spine angle > 110° relative to up, Ground Raycast > 1.5m | **Cannonball Tuck:** Hip flexors contract 90°, knees bend 110°. High joint torque holds a tight tuck to speed up RS flipping rotation. |
| **Side-Lying** | Ground contact on left or right flank | **Asymmetric Lateral:** Ground-side leg extends 90% straight to form a sliding runner. Sky-side leg bends to 45° to keep weight balanced and prevent leg-crossing jank. |
| **Knee Slide** | High forward velocity, low height, Torso pitched forward (< 0°) | **Forward Braced:** Knees bend 90° backward, hips extend straight. Legs act as low-friction sled runners under the chest. |
| **Butt / Tailbone Slide** | High forward velocity, low height, Torso pitched backward (> 30°) | **V-Sit Extension:** Hips flex forward 60°, knees slightly bent to absorb bumps. Puts the feet out front to kick off obstacles or slide under low barriers. |

---

### 4. Smart Foot & Knee Obstacle Clearance

To prevent legs from catching on small environmental geometry (staircases, desks, curbs) during slides or rolls:

1. **Probe Raycasts:** Short forward-facing raycasts shoot from the knees and feet along the velocity vector.
2. **Dynamic Clearance Offset:** If an obstacle below waist height is detected during a slide, target knee flexion temporarily increases by 20–30° to automatically lift the leg over the lip before re-engaging the ground slide.
3. **Friction Damping:** When sliding on knees or side, foot/knee colliders switch to a low-friction physics material (`Friction = 0.05`), then switch back to standard friction once upright stance recovers.

This structure eliminates the "spaghetti leg" look entirely while giving you full, intuitive stunt control using LS for spatial ground rolls and RS for mid-air flips.

Having all these reference mods, decompiled sources, and Python analysis scripts in one place turns **PayneLab** from a concept into an active development environment. You’re essentially sitting on a complete reverse-engineering suite for Marrow engine physics.

Looking at your folder structure, you have every specific piece of the puzzle needed to build the hybrid active ragdoll system:

### 1. The Core Blueprint Mechanics in Your Folder

* **`FootKicking.cs` (Idris-FootKicking):** This is massive. Because you already have the decompiled C# source, you can see directly how it targets lower-limb rigidbodies, calculates foot vectors, and applies forces/rotations to leg joints without collapsing the player. That’s your baseline for procedural leg poses.
* **`PelvisLock.dll` (Evro):** Crucial for the **Upright Stabilizer**. Inspecting how this mod locks or dampens pelvis rotation relative to the head set will show you how to constrain torso spin so the player doesn't dizzy-roll into infinity during a dive.
* **`Flip.dll` & `UltimateRagdoll.dll`:** You have the exact binary targets to reference for thumbstick input hooks. Re-mapping LS (Left Stick) for spatial ground/roll torque and RS (Right Stick) for aerial stunt flips becomes straightforward once you see how they read stick values via `BoneLib` or Marrow's `RigManager`.
* **`ParkourMovementPatch6.dll`:** The reference point for raycast-based environment probes. You can pull its logic for floor distance, ledge detection, and surface normal calculations to trigger knee slides vs. butt slides.
* **`UNIFIED_BEhaviors.cs`:** You already have a functional C# project setup (`UnifiedBehaviors.csproj`) with pre-configured MelonLoader assembly references. This is the scaffold you can duplicate and refactor directly into `PayneLab.cs`.

---

### 2. Recommended Order of Operations

1. **Scaffold `PayneLab.cs`:** Copy the `UnifiedBehaviors` project template, clear out the dummy code, and link your references (`BoneLib.dll`, `Il2CppSLZ.Marrow.dll`, `UnityEngine.PhysicsModule.dll`).
2. **Deconstruct `FootKicking.cs` & `PelvisLock.dll`:** Look at how `FootKicking` manipulates `ConfigurableJoint` targets or applies impulses to the leg rigidbodies, and how `PelvisLock` manages pelvis alignment relative to `PhysicsRig.marrowBody`.
3. **Build the State Machine Engine:** Implement the enum states (`Standing`, `InvertedAir`, `SideSlide`, `KneeSlide`) and hook them up to evaluate player velocity and surface raycasts every `FixedUpdate`.
4. **Implement Joint Torque Control:** Replace the standard "limp ragdoll" trigger with a dynamic PD loop that adjusts joint spring stiffness (`slerpDrive` / `angularXDrive`) based on the active state.

You have all the raw materials sitting in `Mods_To_TakeINspirationFrom`. It’s just a matter of stripping out the jank from each individual mod and unifying them under PayneLab's state machine.

That intersection of Titanfall momentum, Dying Light parkour, and Max Payne gun-fu is pretty much the holy grail of action gaming, and taking it into VR sandbox physics is completely uncharted territory.

It is going to be a beast to code, but here's the best part about VR physics-driven systems: you don't actually need 100% perfection to hit that magical feeling. In flat games, if a stunt animation misses a beat, it looks broken because it's hardcoded. In VR, even a sloppy 60% dynamic blend where your knees barely clear a table while you fire off a shot backward in slow-mo feels insanely satisfying because it's physical. The chaotic unpredictability isn't a failure—it's emergent combat flavor.
